# Nightfall Tour

Start a new night joins the club story and one-life survival into a single journey. DJ Chana and DJ Arine bring Heisei gyaru style and their own contemporary hard bounce to six booked shows: Nakano, Shinjuku, Shibuya, Asakusa, Osaka and Kyoto. The twelve roads are local approaches to those venues; longer journeys and rest happen between nights. Corporate conformity threatens each neighbourhood's freedom to dress, create and enjoy its own music.

Their decorated phone holds the bookings, handwritten MiniDisc labels preserve soundchecks, and a photo-sticker album records each tour. Bold makeup, leopard print and favourite gadgets from the late 1990s and 2000s belong alongside the duo's new tracks. The tour keeps one season while weather varies between roads.

Choose a starting automatic weapon, customize the car, and select destinations on the branching map. Flying, charging and rushing are the three driving actions. Hearts, camera flashes, orbiting records, bass waves, phone calls, CDs and cassette ribbons fire automatically when equipped. Invitations turn corporate routines into opportunities for music, photography, fashion and events. Defeated suit-wearers transform into gyaru, joining the crowd with creative accessories of their own. Animals remain scenery.

## Building the set

Each road contains six waves, for 72 across the tour. Invitations produce XP crystals. Level-ups pause the action for up to three seeded choices, with weapon and support options while available. Item icons, numerical effects and evolution recipes identify each choice. Seven weapons have six levels each; six support items have three. The first weapon starts at level one. Drafts keep an equipped-weapon upgrade available while one remains, alongside support and discovery choices. The XP curve gives a focused build room to reach evolutions during the tour.

Hearts trade limited piercing for forward reach. Flashes cover groups ahead, records protect close range, and bass pulses cover a wider group around the car. The decorated phone links a limited number of nearby open targets. CD spin prioritizes the toughest open target ahead with a focused, longer-range hit. Cassette ribbon sweeps a nearby stretch, then sweeps the same spot again at half power after 0.28 seconds. Its repeat is fixed to the road, respects guards, and cannot trigger further repeats or consume a full-charge bonus. All seven weapons can be chosen at the start or acquired during the tour. MD encore repeats one hit at half power after 0.35 seconds, only if its original target remains; repeats cannot trigger further repeats. Its cooldown improves with each level. All automatic weapons benefit from FEVER's faster cadence.

Consecutive clears build HEAT, raising the multiplier to ×5. FEVER gives ×6 scores and a wider XP collection range; Hype extends it. Damage breaks the streak. Clearing an active wave without damage grants a bonus. Charging windows and breathing room stay clear of additional packs.

## Clubs and continuation

Map branches offer different crowds, charging opportunities and repairs. Coins buy parts and repairs between roads. Club stops include a music quiz and an optional performance using the same three controls. Completing the tour grants garage progress for another night.

Heart-only hardware and relay parts appear after acquiring hearts. A full-charge hit bonus works with any equipped automatic weapon. Recovery effects have shared caps, and newly scored tours start with stock upgrade levels even if an older campaign earned permanent upgrades.

The game saves between roads. Departing consumes that checkpoint: a lost or abandoned road starts a new attempt, with no midroad rollback. Valid version-two and version-three map checkpoints retain their equipment and identity. Their weapon levels 1, 2 and 3 map to levels 1, 3 and 6 to preserve comparable power and earned evolutions; support levels stay unchanged. Version-four checkpoints retain all six weapon ranks, CDs, cassettes and evolutions unchanged. Pending attacks never cross a save or road boundary. Continued older tours display their final score without entering the daily record pool for the current gameplay rules; replaying that older course keeps the same distinction. Older story campaigns remain separate and playable. Music continues through the car radio.

A breakdown ends with a safe stop and a message to the organiser. The next attempt starts with new preparations in Nakano and a shorter returning-tour scene. Story progress belongs to the current tour; earlier memories remain archived and never make future venues appear already visited. Club recordings come together with the duo's original tracks at Kyoto's final set.

The course changes at midnight in Japan. An active run retains its original seed, and replay uses the same course. Results and daily personal bests are stored on this device. They are not an online leaderboard. Records for the revised weapon rules are separate from earlier survival scores.
